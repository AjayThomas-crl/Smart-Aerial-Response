Architecture 2 · Inside the swarm planner

From one flood map and the weather to one validated mission per drone; numbered as in the demo's step strip

Perception and forecast (shared by all drones)

inputFlood map + weatherMap image, rain mm, wind km/h and direction, lat/lon
1Flood mask MResize to 750 px, HSV sampling, 5×5 close/open
1Flood zonesContours ≥ 200 px², convex hulls, centroids
2Spread forecast M̂Dilate 1 px per 5 mm/h, shift 1 px per 10 km/h downwind, 2 h
2No-fly field O = M ∪ M̂Drones and bases must stay out of it

Swarm planning (who flies where)

3Drop pointsOn the dry edge of each zone; 1 to 4 per zone by area, ≥ 50 px apart
3Candidate bases~60×60 grid of dry points with flood clearance
3Routed reachDistance around flood on the D* Lite grid; battery model with payload + headwind
3Greedy base selectionFewest bases (≤ K = 6, ≥ 150 px apart) that cover every reachable drop
3TerritoriesEach drop to its nearest feasible base; unreachable drops flagged for boat teams

Per drone, then airspace check and export

4Visit orderNearest-neighbour tour from the base
4SortiesSplit so payload ≤ 8 kg and battery ≤ 80%; reload at base
4D* Lite per legTime-aware: avoids the flood expected at arrival; refine until stable (≤ 6 rounds)
5DeconflictionAny two drones' routes crossing → reassign or move base (≤ 5 rounds) or refuse
5Export + validatePixel → WGS84, one QGC WPL 110 file per drone, route map, run summary
6Fly the swarmGround control / SITL, or the live command-centre replay
Battery model: Garuda Agri Kisan class drone, 5 m/s, 7 min loaded endurance, 0.25 kg per relief kit, 20% reserve, +8% time per kg payload, +2.5% per m/s headwind.
